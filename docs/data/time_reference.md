# Time zones and reference times

`wetterdienst` returns every `timestamp` as `datetime[us, UTC]`, the same type for every provider and for values,
interpolated values and summaries; the provider tests hold the frame of every result they build to it. Providers do not
publish that way. They stamp in UTC, in local civil time, in local standard time or in a fixed offset, and they stamp
the start, the end or the middle of the period an aggregate covers. A mistake is an hour off, or a day at a daily
boundary, and never an error, so this page records what each provider's own documentation says and what the library
does with it.

How to read the tables:

- **source time zone**: the zone in which the provider states its stamps.
- **reference time**: where the stamp sits in the period a value covers. `instant` is a reading at the stamp, `start`
  and `end` an aggregate over the period that begins or ends at it, `middle` one centred on it, and `mixed` that the
  datasets, parameters or periods of the row differ (the qualifier says how; a row that gives its answer for some of
  the parameters only and leaves the others unqualified or unverified is `mixed`). A daily stamp is mostly a date at
  00:00 UTC, and for it `start` and `end` say that the day's window begins or ends on that date, and the qualifier gives the window.
- **`unverified`** means the provider's documentation does not state it. The cell never holds a guess; where a
  comparison with a second source suggests an answer, the cell says so in brackets as an observation.
- **verified**: `yes` if the quoted text defines both zone and reference time as shown, `partly` if it defines only one
  of them or one rests on the stamp's format or the field names alone, `no` if the documentation defines neither (both
  cells are then `unverified`).
- The notes list, per row, what the library does with the stamp and the sentences of the provider the row rests on.
  German, French, Polish and Spanish quotations are given in the original.

A daily stamp is the date of the day it belongs to, at 00:00 UTC, for most providers, whatever window the provider
defines for that day (06:00 to 06:00 UTC for precipitation is common) and whatever zone the provider keeps its days
in, so that a request for one day (`start` and `end` the same date, which are 00:00 UTC) returns that day's values
where it is so (GH-2683). A monthly stamp is the first of the month at 00:00 UTC. The notes say where it is not.

Rows with a confirmed deviation from their source link the issue that tracks it, and the issue is:

- [#3029](https://github.com/earthobservations/wetterdienst/issues/3029): hourly values stamped in MEZ that no `Zusatz-Info` marks: the soil temperature of the eMD periods and the air temperature of Diepholz from 1995-02-01 to 1996-05-31, Nürnberg from 1986-01-01 to 1988-10-31, Frankfurt/Main in 1990-05, Trier-Petrisberg in 1989-08 and Bremen from 1990-11-01 to 1991-02-28 were returned one hour late and are shifted to UTC with `zone:mez` (the other plain MEZ periods of 111 stations with an ISD twin are UTC in 6,021 of the months ISD tells apart; 86 months at Regensburg-Oberhub, Stötten, Lübeck-Blankensee, Großer Arber and Bad Hersfeld it reads as MEZ, and they are not settled, but for the months of the second source of #3776); the plain MEZ periods of Regensburg-Oberhub and Stötten change their clock inside the label and are not settled, and the hourly wind at Bückeburg and Celle is right as written (NOAA ISD is the one an hour off there)
- [#3776](https://github.com/earthobservations/wetterdienst/issues/3776): the analog plain MEZ periods of the hourly air temperature and humidity at 160 stations were scanned month by month with two methods, an ISD offset fit and a diurnal-phase test (ISD exists from 1955, three-hourly before 1973); 808 station-months at 26 stations (1955 to 1997, almost all before 1991) that both call MEZ under each of the six one-at-a-time variants of the scan's constants (a full grid of all combinations keeps 748) are shifted to UTC and flagged `zone:mez`, those of the 1973-01 to 1975-12 batch at 13 stations `zone:mez-1973-75`; the other months, the ones one method alone calls MEZ among them, are returned as written (the table is `mez_blocks.csv`, the method and the batch are on the page of dwd/observation hourly)
- [#2711](https://github.com/earthobservations/wetterdienst/issues/2711): the MEZ and MOZ periods of the subdaily and daily observations were compared with NOAA ISD and GHCN-daily on samples, and the daily datasets besides `climate_summary` with the hourly ones and with ISD's present weather (the notes of `dwd` give the results). Subdaily air temperature, 53 stations compared, 41 of them judged for 1987 to 2001-03-31: the terms of 27 (Lahr from 1995 on) equal ISD's report of the stamp's UTC hour in every year compared, whatever the label (99 % of the values); those of 13 (and Lahr in 1989) do not, and their clock is not shown, except at the secular station Potsdam, which reads local mean time up to 1991-10; other stations were not measured. At the West German stations compared the reading precedes the stamp hour by a fraction of an hour in the MEZ periods of 1947 to 1986 (median 0.1 to 0.4 h, near local mean time in some years and near the UTC hour in others) and by about 1 h up to 1946 (3-hourly ISD only, one source); the East German MOZ periods of 1964 to 1966 fit local mean time at 14 and 21. Nothing is shifted. No daily stamp of the parameters compared is a day off
- [#2816](https://github.com/earthobservations/wetterdienst/issues/2816): the hourly sun stamps of the periods DWD calls `Stundenwerte in WOZ` (local true solar time, not UTC) are returned as written, unshifted, and the rows are flagged `zone:woz` in `quality_flags`

A deviation that is suspected but was not reproduced, for want of credentials or of a second source, says so in the note of its row.

The type check on the `timestamp` column of every values result runs in each provider test (`tests/provider/conftest.py`).

## The change of clocks

On 2026-10-25 the clocks of Germany, France and the other countries on CEST went back at 01:00 UTC (03:00 CEST to
02:00 CET), so the local hour 02:00 to 02:59 happened twice: first at +02:00 (00:00 to 01:00 UTC), then at +01:00
(01:00 to 02:00 UTC). A feed that stamps local time without an offset would show that hour twice, or skip one on the
spring day; a feed that stamps in UTC or with an offset shows an ordinary hour. The feeds that keep only a short
window were read on 2026-10-26 ([#3137](https://github.com/earthobservations/wetterdienst/issues/3137)), with the
library and, where it matters, the raw file; a feed that no longer held the change by then is marked as not caught.

Per feed, what was read and what it showed:

**wsv/pegel**: the `measurements.json` of the raw API and the library's answer for it: stage every 15 minutes at BONN and DRESDEN, air temperature every 15 minutes at UELZEN and every hour at LÜNEBURG and PASSAU DONAU, stage every minute at CUXHAVEN STEUBENHÖFT and HELGOLAND BINNENHAFEN. The stamps carry their offset, and the local 02:00 to 02:45 is there twice, as `+02:00` (00:00 to 00:45 UTC) and as `+01:00` (01:00 to 01:45 UTC); the library turns them into two different UTC stamps. Continuous in UTC at every station, none repeated or missing (33 values at 15 minutes from 22:00 UTC to 06:00 UTC, 9 hourly, 240 at one minute from 00:00 to 04:00 UTC), and every library value equals the source's at its UTC stamp. The value pattern fits UTC too (read on 2026-11-08): a tide fit with a surge spline over 30 days of the raw 1-minute stage, with the stamps after the change shifted by -60, 0 and +60 minutes, has the least error at no shift at Cuxhaven (rms 37.3, 33.8 and 39.6 cm), Helgoland, Wittdün and Büsum, and the M2 phase moves by 2 to 5 degrees across the change (an hour is 29 degrees). The air temperature of Waren, Potsdam and Uelzen fits a DWD 10-minute station 0.8 to 6.6 km away with the same lag before and after the change, within 10 minutes; Schwerin's fit is flat between 0 and +60 minutes, so it decides nothing.

**eaufrance/hubeau**: the API's `observations_tr` stage at 5 minutes for Brest (J334000101, the tide gauge), Le Verdon-sur-Mer (S100001001) and Honfleur (H704011001), and the library's answer for the first two. Stamps end in `Z`, none repeated, 49 values at 5 minutes from 00:00 to 04:00 UTC at each, no gap in that window; the library equals the API (in centimetres where the API says millimetres). The tide agrees with the stamp: a harmonic fit (M2, S2, N2, K2, K1, O1, M4, MS4) of 12 to 24 October (until 22:00 UTC) predicts the following two days to 0.12 m at Brest (tidal range 6.1 m), and to 1.05 m or 1.02 m if the stamps after 01:00 UTC are moved by -1 or +1 hour; Le Verdon 0.21 m against 0.78 and 0.69 m; Honfleur 0.56 m against 1.15 and 1.54 m (a river reach). Nothing is left open.

**dwd/mosmix**: the MOSMIX-L runs of Frankfurt/Main (10637) issued 24 October 09, 15, 21 UTC and 25 October 03, 09 UTC, the listing of the 48 MOSMIX-S runs (24 October 07 UTC to 26 October 06 UTC), and the library's answer for the runs of 21 UTC and 03 UTC. `IssueTime` and every `TimeStep` end in `Z`; each run holds 247 hourly steps, none missing or repeated; the runs before and after the change give the same curve at the same UTC steps (the minimum of 25 October at 23:00 UTC is 8.2, 7.9, 7.6, 8.2 and 8.1 °C, the maximum about 13 °C between 12:00 and 14:00 UTC in all five). MOSMIX-S has one run for every hour from 00:00 to 04:00 UTC of 25 October, the file names and the server's modification times in UTC. The library returns the same steps and values (kelvin as °C). No offset such as `+01:00` occurs anywhere in these files. Not checked: MOSMIX-S values (37 MB a run).

**dwd/dmo**: the ICON runs of 10637, the 78 h run (hourly) and the 168 h run (every 3 hours), issued 24 October 12 UTC and 25 October 00 and 12 UTC, and the library's answer for the first two. `Z` stamps only; 78 and 31 steps, unique and evenly spaced; the runs agree across the change (the minimum of 25 October at 23:00 UTC is 13.1, 13.7 and 13.4 °C, the maximum is 20.1, 20.7 and 19.6 °C between 13:00 and 14:00 UTC); the library returns the steps and values of the file.

**dwd/alerts**: five district snapshots (the German-language ones) of 24 October 23:59 UTC to 25 October 03:00 UTC, two of 25 October 12:01 and 18:00 UTC, and the library's answer for four timestamps. The CAP stamps are local time with their offset: `onset` has `2026-10-25T02:00:00+02:00` (00:00 UTC) and `2026-10-25T02:00:00+01:00` (01:00 UTC) as different values, no `03:xx+02:00` or `01:xx+01:00` occurs, `sent` after the change carries `+01:00` and lies within a minute of the snapshot's UTC name. The library returns every stamp as `Datetime(us, UTC)` and the doubled hour as two instants. A request for `02:05+01:00` is served the snapshot of 01:03:58 UTC, one for `02:05+02:00` that of 23:59:48 UTC, one for `03:05+01:00` that of 01:56:48 UTC. Not checked: a `sent` stamp between 00:00 and 03:00 UTC of that day, as no alert was issued then (the last `sent` before the change is 22:26 UTC).

**dwd/poi**: not caught. The `10637-BEOB.csv` holds the last 24 hours, and the one served on 26 October (modified 06:38 UTC) starts at 06:00 UTC of 25 October, after the change. Its 25 rows are hourly, unique and in UTC. The next chance is the spring change on 2027-03-28 (01:00 UTC), when the file holds it for 24 hours.

**ipma/observation**: not caught. `observations.json` holds 24 hourly keys, and the one served on 26 October (modified 06:35 UTC) starts at 07:00 UTC of 25 October. The 24 keys are consecutive, 227 stations each. Winter time in Portugal is UTC, so these hours cannot tell UTC from local time; the check of 2026-10-10, in summer time, found UTC (see the note of the row). The next chance is 2027-03-28.

**nws/observation**: not checkable yet. The clocks of the United States go back on 2026-11-01 (02:00 EDT to 01:00 EST, 06:00 UTC). The endpoint keeps about a week, so read it from 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-08: the stamps carry an offset (`+00:00` so far), so the expectation is hourly rows in UTC with no gap or repeat at 05:00 to 08:00 UTC.
